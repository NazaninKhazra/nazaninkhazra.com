Teaching the Frontier

Guest lectures in my courses, taught by PhD students

Each term I set aside a few guest-lecture and tutorial slots in my undergraduate and MA courses for topics outside my own expertise. PhD students, from Economics or any other department, apply to teach one. You get classroom experience; my students get a specialist on a topic I would otherwise cover less well.

Several of my former teaching assistants have used a guest lecture in my courses for their teaching portfolios and job applications. This page makes that a standing offer.

A session is one 60–80 minute lecture or a tutorial tied to a core module of the course. When there is room, I open the session to other undergraduates as well.

Apply to teach a session

What I offer

  • Teaching experience with an undergraduate or MA class, on a topic you know well.
  • A written teaching evaluation. I attend your session and send you written feedback afterwards.
  • A reference. Having seen you teach, I can write a letter for teaching-focused positions.
  • A low-stakes setting to try a format or materials and collect student feedback.

Open topics

Sessions build on the existing syllabus, so yours should connect to what students already know. If you work on something related that is not listed, propose it.

Topics rotate by term. Availability depends on the course calendar and on how a proposed session fits the students' current level.

How it works

  1. Check the open topics above, which I update each term.
  2. Apply. The form asks for the topic, a paragraph on your approach, and how it connects to the course.
  3. Plan. If selected, we meet once to match the session to the students' level and settle materials and timing.
  4. Teach. You give the lecture or tutorial. I attend.
  5. Feedback. You receive a written evaluation, and I can act as a reference afterwards.

Who can apply

PhD students in Economics or any other department whose work relates to an open topic. No prior lecturing experience is needed. Advanced MA students may be considered for tutorial slots.
